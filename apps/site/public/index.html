The gateway between Meta and your systems

A business connects its own WhatsApp Business Account through Embedded Signup. From that moment, Eccos does two things on its behalf.

Receives and forwards

It takes the events Meta sends about the account — incoming messages, delivery and read receipts — and forwards them to an endpoint the business chooses, retrying when that endpoint is down.

Sends

It sends the messages the business asks it to send — including templates and media — through the official Cloud API.

Connect with Embedded Signup

Businesses run WhatsApp support and notifications from their own systems, without a third-party platform sitting between them and Meta. The account stays theirs; conversation charges are billed by Meta directly.

Built so you own it

Your account. Businesses connect their own WhatsApp Business Account through Embedded Signup and pay Meta directly for conversations.

Your infrastructure. The full gateway is open source under the MIT licence — deploy it as a single Bun binary or entirely on Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects, on infrastructure you control.

Your data. Each business's data is isolated — never combined across businesses, never sold, and never used for advertising or to train models.

How your data is handled

In the hosted version, data obtained from the WhatsApp Cloud API — message events, delivery statuses, phone number and account identifiers — is processed for one purpose only: delivering each business its own messages and events.

Data handling Eccos Cloud Self-hosted
Combined or cross-referenced across businesses Never Never
Sold, rented, or shared with data brokers Never Never
Used for advertising or to train models Never Never
Message content retention 30 days by default, configurable 7–90 You decide
Storage jurisdiction Pinned to Cloudflare's European Union jurisdiction Your infrastructure
Sent to our servers Only each business's own events, to deliver them Nothing at all

Eccos is a pass-through gateway, not an archive — your own systems remain your system of record. The full detail, including subprocessors and the caveats we chose to declare rather than round away, is in the Privacy Policy.

Self-hosted deployments send us nothing at all: the data never leaves the infrastructure the business itself operates.
